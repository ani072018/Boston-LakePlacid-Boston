Adirondack Northway Scenic Drive: A Road Wrapped in Autumn's Glow

The road wound through dense forests dressed in every shade of fall — fiery reds, burnt oranges, and glowing yellows that seemed to stretch endlessly. Sunlight filtered through the canopy, scattering gold across the highway, while crisp mountain air filled the car with the scent of pine and earth.

"It was one of those drives where time didn't matter — just the feeling of freedom, and the anticipation of reaching Lake Placid nestled in the heart of the Adirondack Mountains."

The deeper we drove into the Adirondacks, the quieter it became. The hustle of Boston felt miles away, replaced by the rhythm of rustling leaves and the hum of tires on open road.

VETERAN'S MEMORIAL HWYASCENDING TO SUMMIT

Whiteface Veterans Memorial Highway: Rising Above Autumn's Canvas

We began our ascent up Whiteface Mountain via the Veterans Memorial Highway, one of the most scenic drives in New York. The road winds gracefully through dense forests and gentle hills, each turn revealing a new perspective — trees blazing red, orange, gold, sunlight streaming through branches like a natural kaleidoscope.

The higher we climbed toward Lake Placid, the more expansive the views became — valleys, distant Adirondack peaks, and sparkling lakes below, as if we were moving through a living painting of fall foliage.

Elevation
The Whiteface Veterans Memorial Highway climbs roughly 3,500 feet in under five miles — one of the steepest paved ascents in the Northeast.

SUMMIT — WHITEFACE MOUNTAIN 4,867 FT

Whiteface Mountain Summit: 4,867 Feet with Two Lakes Below

Reaching the top of Whiteface Mountain was a reward in itself. From the 4,867 ft summit, the view was absolutely mesmerizing — two lakes stretched out beneath us, their waters reflecting the brilliant fall colors that surrounded them. Standing there, taking it all in, it was clear the journey from Boston along the Adirondack Northway and up the mountain was just as unforgettable as the summit view itself.
